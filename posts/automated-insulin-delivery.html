Automated Insulin Delivery in 2026: The State of the Loop

By Beyond Diabetes · Digital Infrastructure Chair

Hybrid closed-loop systems — sometimes called "the loop" or AID (Automated Insulin Delivery) — have matured dramatically over the past five years. What was once a DIY movement among technically inclined patients is now the standard of care recommendation for many Type 1 individuals. Here's where things stand.

What "hybrid closed-loop" means

A fully closed-loop system would manage glucose entirely autonomously — no manual boluses, no carb counting. We're not there yet. "Hybrid" means the system automates basal insulin delivery and correction boluses, but still requires the user to announce meals. The "hybrid" qualifier is important context when reading device claims.

The current leaders

The Omnipod 5 and Tandem t:slim X2 with Control-IQ remain the two primary FDA-cleared AID options in the US. The Omnipod 5's tubeless design gives it a distinct ergonomic advantage, particularly for children and active adults. Tandem's Control-IQ technology runs a predictive algorithm that is updatable via software — users received the latest improvements via a download, not a device replacement.

The Medtronic MiniMed 780G has FDA clearance and brings auto-correction boluses every 5 minutes — a more aggressive automation model than some competitors. Its real-world A1c data in European markets has been compelling, though it remains under-utilized in the US due to Medtronic's overall market share decline.

What's still missing

Meal announcement remains the biggest friction point. Research into "fully automated" systems using dual-hormone approaches (insulin + glucagon or pramlintide) is ongoing, but no fully closed-loop single-hormone system has reached broad clinical approval. Speed of insulin action is also a ceiling — faster analogs like Fiasp are helping, but physics limits how reactive any subcutaneous delivery system can be.

The DIY community

Systems like Loop (iOS), AAPS (Android), and OpenAPS continue to be used by a dedicated community. They often offer more configurability than commercial options and have in some cases driven features that commercial devices later adopted. They remain unregulated and require significant technical investment, but for the right person, they represent the cutting edge of AID.
